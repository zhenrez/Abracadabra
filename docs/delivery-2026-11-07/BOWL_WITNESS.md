## What this can and cannot discriminate

The present acquisition does **not** decide inherited rounding, contact-induced rounding, or parallel change. A transcribed waw alone does not establish a precise ancient phoneme, length, or waveform; a broad corpus date does not date one spelling. A verified early phonetic use could constrain the latest time by which the relevant vowel quality existed in that witness, conditional on the transcription, dating, and phonetic interpretation. It would not by itself establish common ancestry or eliminate contact.

**Exact next discriminator:** obtain the identified Num 9:23 editions/lines and confirm the letter reading against photographs or explicit editorial apparatus; obtain the independent dating basis; then assess whether the proposed qameṣ-corresponding vowel letters occur beyond a conditioned vernacular/orthographic environment. Compare ordinary vowel-letter use, possible yod/waw ambiguity, emphatic or pharyngeal contexts, and Hebrew versus Aramaic portions. These are proposed checks, not claims that Molin's unretrieved pages establish those conditions. A genealogical conclusion additionally needs independent cross-tradition chronology/contact evidence.
