# What the rounded-vowel comparison can distinguish

The recovered prior art supplies a concrete test of linguistic relationship, with a consequential limitation. Kantor's reported rounded-qameṣ distribution groups Babylonian and Tiberian against Secunda, Jerome and Palestinian. These are author-reconstructed feature states from different example words, not five measurements of one token.

SUNNY enumerated all 105 rooted, fully branching trees on these five labelled traditions. Assuming an unrounded ancestor, one shared innovation, no reversal and no contact, exactly 15 trees survive. Every survivor contains a Babylonian–Tiberian clade; the remainder of the genealogy stays unresolved.

Allowing two independent innovations makes all 105 trees compatible: one change on each of the two terminal branches suffices. Thus the observed distribution alone cannot distinguish shared descent from independent change. A preference for fewer changes would be an added criterion, not historical evidence.

This tells the investigation what to seek next: chronology that tests a shared change, independently supported additional innovations, and evidence about contact or copying. It prevents a matching sound feature from silently becoming a genealogy, a unique original pronunciation, or a musical frequency.

## Independent verification and next source dependencies

The independent verifier uses successive pair merging rather than recursive partitions, then exhaustively assigns internal binary states with the root fixed at zero. It reproduces the same 105 trees: 15 need one gain and 90 need two. The production helper now forbids an implicit pre-root change. Both commands passed after that correction.

Inspection of Kantor pp78–79 identifies a specific dependency chain: rounding near waw is argued to precede its change from [w] to [v]; the latter is dated through a separate contact study. Babylonian bowl spellings provide another line of evidence. Neither a shared deadline nor shared rounding alone establishes one inherited event.
