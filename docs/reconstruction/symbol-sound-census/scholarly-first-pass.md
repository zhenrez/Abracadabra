# Aleph/A scholarly census, first pass

Question: Which witness-qualified relationships among letter shape, order, phonetic function and acoustic realization are identifiable across Paleo-Hebrew, Aramaic and English, with Greek/Latin comparative controls?

Status: all 14 questions addressed provisionally. Scholarly baseline remains OPEN pending dated early Hebrew, Aramaic and Latin witness work. Do not open power/heterodox triage. No prospective prediction evaluated.

[Unicode 16, chapter 10](https://unicode.org/versions/Unicode16.0.0/core-spec/chapter-10/) defines the implementation scope of the Phoenician block, including Paleo-Hebrew and Early Aramaic. Use to track encoding ambiguity; it does not reconstruct sounds.
[Khan, The Tiberian Pronunciation Tradition, vol. 1 (2020)](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0163.pdf) distinguishes oral reading and written transmission in the introduction, pp. 36-38; the Alef treatment begins in I.1.1 on p. 135. Tiberian evidence requires an explicit chronological transfer argument before application to Paleo-Hebrew.
[Greek Alphabetic Writing](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-ancient-history/greek-alphabetic-writing/E26EF87BAEDA11D809E7F88751C1BE43) gives names, ordering and forms as distinct evidence for descent. Only the public summary was inspected.
[CMUdict](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cmusphinx/cmudict/master/cmudict.dict), observed rows 15-16 (zero-based), gives a AH0 and a(2) EY1. A word-level North American English control, not a letter-by-letter correspondence or a waveform.

**Q01. Who has proposed this or something structurally equivalent before?** Prior art documents alphabet adaptation and the distinction between transmitted orthography and oral reading (Jeffery; Khan). No jurisdiction-wide novelty claim.

**Q02. What fields study this problem?** Comparative epigraphy, palaeography, historical phonology, recitation studies and pronunciation lexicography.

**Q03. What terminology do those fields use for it?** Search terms include aleph/alef/alaph, alpha, glottal stop, reading tradition, abjad, acrophony and grapheme-to-phoneme.

**Q04. What are the closest established theories, models, equations, or frameworks?** Test graphic inheritance separately from phonetic reassignment and contextual pronunciation. Modern encoding is a separate representational layer.

**Q05. What reviews, surveys, bibliographies, encyclopedias, dissertations, or meta-analyses map the space?** Khan volume I and the Cambridge Ancient History chapter give bibliographic entry points. Systematic reviews across the three priority traditions remain unassessed.

**Q06. What are the canonical papers, books, and authors?** Khan 2020 is a direct analysis of Tiberian reading. Retrieve additional primary epigraphic editions for early Hebrew and Aramaic.

**Q07. What are the competing explanations?** Candidates: persistent prescribed sound in a bounded ritual context; contextual realizations; preservation of graphic order with phonetic reassignment. Different scopes can coexist.

**Q08. What has been falsified, criticized, or abandoned?** The unrestricted claim that one written English word-form a fixes one dictionary pronunciation fails against the two CMU entries. This control addresses English lexicon only.

**Q09. What are the unresolved questions?** The original intended coupled sound, a cross-tradition operation and an acoustic reference remain unresolved. No first-millennium vocal recording was inspected.

**Q10. What primary sources or datasets are important to the field?** CMUdict entries a and a(2), Unicode v16 chapter 10, Khan I.1.1 pp.135-149. Early primary Aramaic and Paleo-Hebrew inscribed tokens still needed.

**Q11. What earlier historical traditions have scholars already connected to it?** The Cambridge Ancient History analysis compares West Semitic and Greek alphabetic names, forms and order. Adaptation is evidence for testing continuity dimension by dimension.

**Q12. Has terminology changed over time in ways that hide older versions?** Retrieval under script name, letter name, historical transcription and academic transliteration changes source coverage. Search all aliases with contextual qualifiers.

**Q13. Have adjacent disciplines rediscovered the same structure under another name?** Lexicography maps words to multiple pronunciations; encoding maps characters to identifiers; neither operation supplies a unique melodic frequency. Deliberate methodological analogy.

**Q14. What obvious prior-art searches have produced nothing?** The three initial queries yielded relevant sources. No bounded negative search on ancient acoustic prescriptions yet. No conclusion of absence.
