# Indian native hierarchy: a source-defined working census

The packet contains **2,520 register-qualified melakarta/svara occurrences and 36 source-qualified instrument-type nodes**: **2,556 declared lower units**. This meets the requested 2,500–2,700 range for this bounded, heterogeneous census. It is not a count of distinct physical frequencies, physical instruments or the whole Indian musical universe.

The full graph has **5,339 records and 8,681 relationships in two views**. Structural classes, 504 mela/svara memberships, 144 direction templates, 1,152 ordered scale-path positions and rhythm contexts are counted separately from the 2,556 lower units. Generation and global SUNNY placement remain unassigned.

Unit 5 begins on page 50. Original PDFs and complete Exa connector responses are saved in `state/research/indian-native/`.

These are modern teaching witnesses. Their reports about older texts are not direct readings of Vedic material, the Natyashastra or Dattilam. Carnatic, Hindustani and older witnesses remain distinct. The recovered `melakarta_audit.py` is an exploratory 12-position control; it was not executed or promoted to native authority in this packet.

## Executed native constructions

- **Mela classes:** six R–G choices × six D–N choices × two M classes give 72 finite addresses, grouped in 12 chakras. Source-given co-locations remain co-locations. Equal spacing and concert pitch are unassigned.
- **Register operator:** the source names five sthayi and defines the upper/lower dot rule. Each of 72 mela parents has seven svara memberships, each with five individually parented register occurrences. Each address is `(mela, sthayi, native svarasthana, degree)`. The source's normal-singer extent of three registers remains separate from this formal address space.
- **Direction templates:** ascending and descending eight-position paths retain the tonic return. Their 1,152 positions do not invent another named register or represent performed notes.
- **Rhythmic class growth:** seven suladi families expand to 35 jati-qualified classes and 175 jati/gati contexts, with 500 parented anga positions. Durations are in aksharakalas. Measured seconds and uninspected gati subdivision quantities remain unknown.
- **Instrument units:** 34 classification nodes retain the reported fourfold and modern threefold families, string excitation/stopping, wind supply/blowing, drum striking/faces, tuning, concert role, folk use and adapted-instrument axes. All 38 explicitly named instrument forms in Unit 4 are addressable: 36 type nodes and two unresolved name forms. Nine Tambura parts, two uncertain part-name forms and four registered string roles have separate units. No physical instrument instances are generated.

The instrument census preserves Ektara/Ektar and Sanoor/Santoor as possible correspondences. It does not assert alias merges or count the uncertain forms as two additional types. Fiddle and Violin remain the source's separate labels; no equivalence is forced. Electronic Tambura is retained as the source-named electronic variant of Tambura. The chapter includes Western instruments adapted to Indian music, so classification does not assert Indian origin. Its national figure of 500 instruments is reported on page 41, not enumerated or independently verified here.

These are source-rule expansions and witness-scoped classifications. They do not establish the complete performance identity of a raga, its gamakas, a fixed intonation or a historical connection to Western or Chinese systems.

The two native constructions producing **35** are distinct: **5 sthayi × 7 svara memberships** gives 35 registered note addresses **per mela**, while **7 suladi tala families × 5 laghu jati** gives 35 tala classes. Their operator domains and units remain explicit. Equality of cardinality does not establish a shared generator, global SUNNY generation, or musical phi. A separate Padgett comparison is not admitted as Indian source evidence here.

Raw observations and prose-conditioned addresses remain separate fields; missing digits are not presented as observed ink.

Each of the 210 registered addresses in the last chakra carries its unresolved prose-versus-chart condition directly. The model retains literal `R3/G2` and prose-rule `R3/G3`, and does not select a historical reading.
