# Biblical genealogy reconstruction

The current working corpus separately addresses every one of Theographic's 3,067 named-entity/person records and preserves its family assertions, partners, siblings, alternate names and verse references. Its acquired KJV1769 corpus contains 31,102 verses across 66 books. Source bytes are pinned to the maintainer repository commit and verified against Git blob identities. This is useful whole-Bible reference material, not a completed diplomatic three-witness collation.

The existing SUNNY score contains five received chronology variants: **MT, SP, Greek_430, Greek_400 and Clementine_Latin**. A recovered analytical passage compares Masoretic/Samaritan/Greek accounting families. Three working family views retain their five clock variants. These are unrelated to the three Goetic catalogue counts or A/B/C origin hypotheses. The exact intended three remain a source-recovery qualification.

**Samaritan Pentateuch means Pentateuch.** The SP view does not invent a Samaritan whole Bible or New Testament. Shared comparison data can be inspected, but no curator/KJV relation is automatically promoted into all three original witnesses.

Theographic is reused prior art, with captured maintainer-declared CC BY-SA 4.0 data and KJV public-domain translation text. Its family table and annotated person links are curator interpretations. Its modern Jones-based chronology is retained as unadopted metadata, not substituted for the received clocks. The acquired CSV bytes match the pinned Git blobs at commit `4af2272ffcc5c77621e3877c7567b742adb0824f`. Raw source packets, actual prior-art discovery and Supermemory retrieval are under `state/research/biblical-genealogies`.

Actual local Jev receipt 64 directs source-defined expansion before global musical assignment; the parent executed that inference. This module inherits known source placements instead of making them unknown merely because independent manuscript collation is unfinished. It preserves 15 participant placements across all five clocks, including their exact onset, duration, GEN, TIME, source references and conditions. It adds 92 addressed lineage occurrences: MT has 33 named entries Adam–David, Greek has 34, and SP has 25 Adam–Hezron entries licensed only as the inherited Genesis accounting. Their 89 descent links and 10 local clock links remain conditional analytical source claims. Named-entry rank and local received GEN are separately typed; neither supplies a universal generation coordinate.

The full Bible family table remains available alongside those conditional views. A fixed, qualified bridge records each native person role, source verse and exact curator lookup. It never joins all occurrences of a homonymous name. MT/SP retain Arphaxad–Shelah; the expanded Greek chain retains Arphaxad–Cainan II–Shelah. SP receives no Ruth, David or Gospel witness claims.

The original public Swete volume is *The Old Testament in Greek according to the Septuagint*, volume I, Cambridge, **1887**, as its actual title page states. Rendered PDF pages 39–41 correspond to printed pages 7–9; PDF pages 49–50 to printed pages 17–18. Greek OCR is unreliable; those five page images were actually inspected. Publication readings are not independent readings of the underlying manuscripts.

An initial low-resolution apparatus reading was corrected after a higher-resolution render: Swete's Genesis 11:13 main reading is **430**, with **330 at E and 130 at D** in the apparatus. The printed D legend identifies *Codex Cottonianus Geneseos*. The apparatus does **not** corroborate the inherited Greek_400 branch. That branch remains preserved and explicitly awaits its own original-edition source. The source packet retains the exact apparatus, PDF/image hashes and correction, rather than silently changing inherited chronology.

The initial implementation independently reads Matthew1:2–16 begetting clauses and Luke3:23–38 son-of clauses from the supplied verse text. Every list occurrence has its own address; repeated names are not silently merged. Luke's Jesus/Joseph “as was supposed” qualifier and terminal God/Adam relation are explicit. Matthew1:16 contributes Mary's birth relation and Joseph/Mary partnership rather than a fabricated “Joseph begat Jesus” clause. Genealogical begetting language is not forced into one immediate biological generation per listed entry.

This separation exposes a real source-layer discrepancy: the curated table makes Heli a father of Mary, whereas Luke3:23's acquired wording places Heli after Joseph. Both claims remain visible, with their different provenance; no harmonization is selected. The curator's global Arphaxad–CainanII–Salah chain also cannot be projected unchanged into MT/SP Genesis11. The retained expanded Lukan archive and five chronology variants preserve that difference.

God-as-father/creation assertions, ordinary curated parentage, explicit textual genealogy, partnership and sibling claims have distinct types. An unnamed wife of Cain receives a verse-addressed anonymous identity rather than a guessed name. Unknown source identities and chronology remain unknown. There is no inferred succession, instrument, phase, pitch, universal generation rank or historical orientation.

Reproduce by running `extract_genesis_witnesses.py`, then `bind_genesis_witnesses.py` under the source-packet directory, then `scripts/reconstruct_biblical_genealogies.py`, using the research Python environment. No network is used during these builds. The current output has **3,279 entities, 4,735 relationships, 8,924 curator field assertions, 124 independently extracted KJV textual claims, 49 targeted edition/transcription begetting claims, and 15 inherited clock placements**. Here “independently extracted” distinguishes verse parsing from the family CSV; it does not mean independent manuscript witnesses. IDs that existed before the added witness layer are unchanged.

No universal phonetic, biological, historical or musical law is claimed.
