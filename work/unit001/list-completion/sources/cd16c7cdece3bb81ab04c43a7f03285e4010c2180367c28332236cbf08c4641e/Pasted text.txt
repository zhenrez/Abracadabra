# 4. ARCHITECTURE DECISION: CONSOLIDATED CORE

The old matrix-specific `WitnessRecord` must NOT become the universal ontology.

Use a small, domain-independent evidence kernel, with specialized typed domain records.

## Layer A — Immutable source vault

Define a `SourceArtifact` or equivalent containing:

- stable source ID;
- original content or content-addressed reference;
- content hash;
- exact encoding;
- source location;
- version or revision;
- retrieval context;
- license and permitted use;
- acquisition metadata;
- source lineage;
- supersession information.

Store original input bytes where available.

Never rewrite original content through Unicode normalization, transliteration, IPA correction, or editorial cleanup.

## Layer B — Evidence and provenance kernel

Define six stable conceptual contracts:

1. `SourceArtifact`
2. `EvidenceObservation`
3. `EvidenceClaim`
4. `DerivationRecord`
5. `OperatorSpecification`
6. `ExperimentReceipt`

Provide deterministic serialization, stable identity, referential integrity, provenance traversal, and append-only history.

Use W3C PROV's entity/activity/agent distinctions as conceptual guidance where useful.

Do not require RDF infrastructure merely to satisfy this checkpoint.

## Layer C — Specialized evidence domains

Design extensible records for:

**Epigraphy:** sign type, sign occurrence, artifact, image region, orientation, damage, actual writing surface, uncertain transcription, alternatives.

**Phonetics and language:** phoneme, allophone, pronunciation tradition, dialect, IPA transcription, root, pattern, affix, cognate judgment, borrowing, inheritance.

**Textual witnesses:** manuscript, edition, recension, written sequence, reading variant, translation, commentary, text-span alignment.

**Rabbinic evidence:** Qere/Ketiv, pointing, dagesh, mappiq, Shin/Sin, Resh, articulatory classes, dialect observations, scribal anomalies, hermeneutic and legal interpretations.

**Diagrammatic witnesses:** historical Tree-of-Life diagrams, visible nodes and edges, spatial geometry, inscriptions, different layouts, order, direction, containment, uncertainty.

**Mathematics and music:** exact quantities, units, generators, state spaces, registers, projections, compositions, residuals.

**Narratives and history:** agents, narrated events, manuscript attestations, chronology claims, source dependencies, competing transmission graphs.

**Normative evidence:** actors, actions, obligations, permissions, prohibitions, conditions, exceptions, authority and period.

These are representational contracts, not authorization to implement the downstream analytic engines.

## Layer D — Typed operator registry

Separate the mathematical definition of an operation from claims that it occurred historically.

An `OperatorSpecification` must declare:

- input and output types;
- precise domain and codomain;
- parameters;
- preconditions;
- transformation rule;
- partiality;
- reversibility or injectivity relative to its domain;
- exact mathematical invariants where applicable;
- validation method;
- implementation/version.

A separate `TransformationClaim` links a specific observed difference to a proposed operator with source evidence and uncertainty.

For example, executing Atbash establishes the operation's result. It does not establish that a historical author intentionally used Atbash.

Support registration of transformation definitions without enabling unrestricted search or cross-orientation inference.

Do not store provisional MDL bit costs as factual properties of operators. Those costs depend on the later coding scheme.

## Layer E — Validation infrastructure

Define a common experiment contract for:

- target data;
- source dependencies;
- train/test separation;
- known-original controls;
- model version;
- sealed predictions;
- evaluation metrics;
- negative controls;
- error and abstention;
- final evidence receipts.

Implement only the G0/G1 functionality needed to validate these contracts now.

# 7. PRIORITIZE HISTORICAL SOURCES CORRECTLY

When investigating historical claims:

1. Prefer the earliest relevant **surviving attestations**, not automatically the oldest surviving edition of a later work.
2. Preserve alternative readings and different textual lineages.
3. Record date of composition separately from date of surviving manuscript.
4. Record editions, restorations, translations, and commentaries separately.
5. Preserve later witnesses where they contain information absent from earlier witnesses.
6. Do not count copied or derivative witnesses as independent confirmation.
7. Do not treat a modern theological or occult reconstruction as an ancient source.
8. Do not treat graphological similarity or numerical agreement as proof of transmission.

A later source can be crucial evidence about a later tradition without being evidence that the same practice existed in an earlier period.
