# P10 — Sequence reversal family

For an ordered sequence of length \(n\), define:

\[
J_n(x)_j=x_{n-1-j}.
\]

Then:

\[
J_n^2=\mathrm{id}.
\]

The already-proved serial retrograde is exactly:

\[
R_{\rm retro}=J_{12}
\]

on twelve-position pitch-class rows.

For a Messiaen non-retrogradable rhythm \(w\), the defining invariant is:

\[
J_n(w)=w.
\]

Therefore these two source objects do not require two unrelated primitive mechanisms.

The relation is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{serial retrograde}=\text{reversal action},
\qquad
\text{non-retrogradation}=\text{fixed-point predicate of reversal}.
}
\]

So:

- reversal remains an action;
- non-retrogradation does not enter the generator basis independently.

This is a **generator-family / invariant relation**, not identity of musical objects.

# P11 — Octave-shift equivalence is generated by SUN closure

SUN's scalar closure is:

\[
O=RL=\frac12.
\]

Therefore:

\[
O^{-1}=2.
\]

An octave frequency shift is multiplication by \(2\).

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{octave shift}=O^{-1}
}
\]

on the positive-frequency realization.

Under:

\[
d(r)=\log_2r,
\]

this becomes:

\[
d(O^{-1})=+1.
\]

Therefore an octave-equivalence quotient may be represented as quotienting by the cyclic action generated by the already-constructible closure inverse.

Consequence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{octave-shift equivalence does not require a new SUN scalar generator}.
}
\]

Carrier/unitization remain explicit: the equivalence relation on a pitch representation is not literally the same object as the multiplicative frequency action.

# P12 — Pitch-class transposition/inversion equivalence are quotient relations over existing actions

The serial proof already supplies:

\[
T_a(x)=x+a\pmod{12},
\]

and:

\[
I(x)=-x\pmod{12}
\]

with translated inversion axes represented by \(T_aI\).

Pitch-class set theory records transposition and inversion **equivalence** as distinct quotient criteria.

Therefore the quotient relations are induced by already-qualified actions:

\[
A\sim_T B
\iff
\exists a:\ B=T_a(A),
\]

and:

\[
A\sim_I B
\iff
\exists a:\ B=T_aI(A).
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{transposition equivalence and inversion equivalence are not additional generators}
}
\]

once \(T\) and \(I\) are present.

The same rule applies to CQT/OPTIC/orbifold representations where transposition/inversion are explicitly listed among quotient transformations: the quotient operation changes the represented space but does not introduce a new underlying action family merely by being used as an equivalence criterion.

# P13 — Representation quotient and action must remain distinct

Even where an equivalence relation is induced by an action:

\[
x\sim_G y
\iff
\exists g\in G:\ g\cdot x=y,
\]

the quotient:

\[
X/G
\]

is not the generator \(g\), not the action \(G\curvearrowright X\), and not the original carrier \(X\).

Therefore SUN must keep three nodes/roles distinct:

1. action/generator;
2. induced equivalence relation;
3. quotient representation.

This prevents repeated counting of one transformation family as several “alphabet letters.”
