**Date:** 2026-09-30

# Core scalar predictions

## P-SUN-11 — 3-limit lattice prediction

For any system claimed to instantiate the same scoped multiplicative kernel as SUN:

\[
R=\frac23,\qquad L=\frac34,
\]

represent each ratio by its prime-exponent vector:

\[
\nu(2^a3^b)=(a,b).
\]

Because:

\[
\nu(R)=(1,-1),\qquad
\nu(L)=(-2,1)
\]

form a unimodular basis, SUN predicts that every ratio generated by the kernel lies in:

\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z^2}
\]

and conversely every 3-limit ratio:

\[
2^a3^b
\]

is generated by \(R,L\) and inverses.

### Falsification
A claimed same-kernel scalar operation requiring a prime other than 2 or 3, or an irrational ratio, is not generated by this kernel without an explicit extension.

---

## P-SUN-12 — Minimal scalar rank

The scalar kernel predicts:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}=2}.
\]

One generator cannot generate the full 3-limit multiplicative lattice.

Therefore any claimed exact reduction of both \(R\) and \(L\) to powers of one scalar generator must fail unless the operation vocabulary is changed.

---

## P-SUN-13 — Closure is constructible, not primitive

\[
O=RL=\frac12.
\]

Therefore the octave/generation closure element is generated by the two scalar primitives.

SUN predicts:

\[
\boxed{O\text{ is not an independent scalar generator}}
\]

inside this algebra.

---

## P-SUN-14 — Reciprocal dual is internal

Because inverses are in the group:

\[
R^{-1}=\frac32,\qquad
L^{-1}=\frac43.
\]

SUN predicts that the expansion pair and contraction pair belong to one generated algebra, not two unrelated systems.

---

## P-SUN-15 — Root-extension test

An equal-step ratio such as:

\[
q=2^{1/n}
\]

is outside the rational 3-limit lattice for \(n>1\), while:

\[
q^n=2
\]

may land inside it.

Therefore SUN predicts a precise distinction:

\[
\boxed{\text{root extension} \neq \text{native lattice element}}
\]

even when the extension closes onto a generated octave.

A proposed external system can therefore match SUN's closure while still requiring a genuinely new local generator.

# Group-action predictions

## P-SUN-16 — Dihedral action normal form

A system with one order-\(12\) cyclic displacement \(s\) and one involution \(t\) satisfying:

\[
s^{12}=e,\qquad
t^2=e,\qquad
tst=s^{-1}
\]

instantiates:

\[
\boxed{D_{12}}
\]

of order 24.

SUN's comparison protocol predicts that such systems should admit a two-generator normal form:

\[
s^at^b,
\qquad
a\in\mathbb Z_{12},\ b\in\mathbb Z_2.
\]

Serial \(T/I\) and neo-Riemannian PLR are current music witnesses.

### Falsification
If the candidate action has different order, fails the conjugation relation, or is not faithful on its declared carrier, it is not the same action family.

---

## P-SUN-17 — Independent reversal doubling

If a \(D_{12}\) action receives a new involution \(r\) that:

1. commutes with the \(D_{12}\) action;
2. is not generated by the \(D_{12}\) subgroup;

then SUN predicts:

\[
\boxed{
G\cong D_{12}\times C_2
}
\]

and:

\[
|G|=48.
\]

This is exactly the recovered serial case, where pitch-class \(T/I\) supplies \(D_{12}\) and retrograde supplies the independent \(C_2\).

### Falsification
If the added involution is already inside the dihedral subgroup, the order does not double.

---

## P-SUN-18 — Same group does not imply same action

If two systems both realize \(D_{12}\), SUN predicts only an abstract generator-family equivalence unless an action-conjugacy receipt also maps their carriers.

Thus:

\[
G_A\cong G_B
\]

does **not** imply:

\[
(X_A,G_A)\cong(X_B,G_B).
\]

This explicitly predicts cases where algebra matches but musical semantics do not.

## P-SUN-20 — Undefined composition is expected

Because the global composition contract is partial, SUN predicts that some source-qualified operations will be genuinely:

\[
\boxed{\text{NONCOMPOSABLE}}
\]

across carriers or semantic types.

A universal composition table would therefore count against the current model unless backed by explicit adapters.

---

## P-SUN-21 — Cross-carrier matches require adapters

For native carriers \(X_s\) and \(X_t\), no cross-system operator identity is inferred merely from equal formulas.

A valid cross-carrier match must exhibit an adapter:

\[
\phi:X_s\rightarrow X_t
\]

and a commuting or otherwise explicitly scoped relation between actions.

This predicts that many numerical matches will downgrade to:

- cardinality only;
- evaluated equality;
- representation equivalence;
- or no match.

# Testing protocol

For every new domain:

1. derive its native carrier and operations independently;
2. freeze them;
3. state the SUN prediction before mapping;
4. construct only lawful adapters;
5. test algebra, arity, path, address, unitization, and residual;
6. retain failures.

Allowed results remain:

- EXACT_STRUCTURAL_MATCH
- SCOPED_ISOMORPHISM
- REPRESENTATION_EQUIVALENT
- GENERATOR_FAMILY_EQUIVALENCE
- PARTIAL_CLIP
- CARDINALITY_ONLY
- COUNTEREXAMPLE
- NO_MATCH
- NOT_EVALUABLE
