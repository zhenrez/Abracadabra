What the historical witnesses constrain

Aleph. The pinned WLC edition encodes a dot on aleph in exactly six word occurrences across its 39 book files. Four belong to the traditional list; the additional two are in Ruth 2:10 and 2:11. The underlying leaves still need direct word-level comparison. The distribution alone supplies no duration or frequency for the consonant.

Every occurrence and book fingerprint · Reading instructions and competing interpretations · Locate the Ruth readings

Ayin. At Hebrew Psalm 22:25 the editor places the Greek ending αυεω on the surviving side of the restoration bracket. The epsilon has two proposed explanations: it can help represent the syllable structure or approximate the guttural sound. Both explanations fit this spelling. Distinguishing them requires additional transcriptions where syllable structure and guttural presence vary independently.
